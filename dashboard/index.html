Step 1

Send some traffic

To spot a problem, the system first needs to see what normal looks like. Fake customers hit the checkout page, and every request is recorded as a trace.

What's sampling?

Real systems can't keep every trace. Here each trace is kept or dropped as a whole, decided by a number derived from its trace ID, so all services agree without talking to each other. Failed and slow traces are always kept as evidence.

What's a trace?

A trace is the diary of one request: which services it visited, in what order, and how long each took. Services pass a trace ID along with every call, so the pieces can be stitched together.

Step 2

Break something

Pick a failure. It is injected into the real service or database, just like a bad deploy or an overloaded server would do.

Is this really breaking things?

In this hosted demo the failure is simulated: the simulator makes that service slower or flakier, and the real analysis engine then has to find it, exactly as it would with real services.

Is this really breaking things?

Yes. Slow-downs really sleep inside the service or database. For Postgres, queries genuinely take longer inside the database; for Redis, the server genuinely stalls. Undo removes it.

Step 3

See the symptom

This is what a customer feels: the checkout page. It tells you that something is wrong, but not where. That's the hard part of debugging microservices.

What is p95?

p95 is the time that 95 out of 100 requests beat. Averages hide pain; p95 shows what the unlucky customers experience.

Step 4

Find the cause

Every service reports how long it spent on its own work versus waiting on others. A service that is merely waiting is a victim. The one whose own time exploded is the culprit.

How does it decide?

It compares each service's own time with the 40 seconds before the fault, flags real jumps, then follows the call graph downwards: blame goes to the deepest service that is itself broken. Details are in docs/03-rca-engine.md.

Step 5

Check the proof

Don't just trust the answer. See the five pieces of evidence behind it, a real slow request step by step, and the error messages the service logged.

Why are logs hidden by default?

Logs are noisy. The system only reads error logs from the suspects, and groups identical messages, so you see the signal first.
